Earned Trust Review

Stop asking a human to read every line an agent wrote. Give each change a Trust Dossier.

🤖 The AI

  • Runs the change and reads everything, and proves what it claims
  • Says what it refused to decide and what it did not check
  • Points to the few hunks that carry the risk

👤 A named engineer

  • Reads those hunks
  • Answers the open questions
  • Signs for the risk that remains. No signature, no merge

How much the engineer must read depends on rules and on each code area's track record. For the people using it there are three steps: a pull request opens, the review posts the dossier, the owner signs.

How a change moves through review · one pipeline, one comment
  1. RequirementFetched with its source. Only the issue author and maintainers can set binding criteria.
  2. Evidence runThe change is built and run on real inputs, before and after, on a machine with no secrets.
  3. Risk triageNamed rules give each unit a tier: T0, T1 or T2.
  4. Six lensesIntent, correctness, security, compatibility, docs, verifiability. In parallel.
  5. VerifierTries to refute every claim. Checks each quoted line against the code.
  6. Trust DossierOne comment, readable in 10 seconds. A summary goes in the PR description.
  7. 👤 Owner signsWith a comment on the PR. The merge is blocked until then.
  8. LedgerReviews, sign-offs and later outcomes: reverts, incidents, escapes.

👤 marks the one step a human does. The AI and scripts do the rest.

The ledger feeds step 3. An area with a clean record earns a lighter tier. One escape withdraws it.

How trust is measured: from a ledger of reviews, sign-offs and later outcomes, not from review activity. The bar is that owner minutes fall while reverts and incidents do not rise. Measured so far: the share of lines the owner must read, 3.5% to 30%.

Principles: how we balance the five tensions

TensionOur ruleHow it is enforced
RiskDepth of human reading follows risk, by rules a person can read.Every tier names its rule id. The model may raise a tier, never lower it.
HallucinationThe AI checks the AI, and scripts check both.A script compares every quoted path:line with the code. A verifier tries to refute each finding. From the evidence run, only what scripts measured is used, never an agent's own summary.
TransparencySay what was not done, as clearly as what was.Fixed sections: what was not checked and why, what the AI refused to decide and who decides.
ConfidenceConfidence is the kind of evidence, never a score.E1 executed, E2 traced with checked citations, E3 inferred. A blocking finding needs E2 or better.
QualityTests must prove the change, not just exist.New tests are run without the change; those that still pass are flagged. Changed lines that no run reached are listed.

Above all of these: the AI gives advice with evidence, never approval. No output says "approved". The merge stays blocked until the named owner signs. The owner can overrule the AI, but has to say why.

Trade-offs we chose

  • A human signature blocks every merge.Gave up: auto-merge speed. Each change waits 15 to 18 minutes for the review, then for a person.
  • Evidence class instead of a confidence score.Gave up: some real bugs arrive as questions, because a blocker needs E2 or better.
  • Rules decide the tier; the model can only raise it.Gave up: precision. Rules are coarse, so the owner still read 3.5% to 30% of the changed lines.
  • Run the change, and verify every claim.Gave up: cost. A build, runs before and after, six lenses and a verifier per change. We have not measured the price per review.

Limits

  • A reference implementation, not a product. It knows one codebase, a C# library. No frontend sample yet.
  • Nothing runs on the reported version. On #2234 the headline rests on reading the version history (E2), not on a run.
  • Trust needs history. The ledger holds four reviews and no outcomes yet, so every area is still neutral.
  • The AI is not independent of itself. The lenses and the verifier are one model family, and the reviewing agent runs unattended with an API key.

What the owner signs

  1. What was checked, and by whom or what
  2. What was not checked, and why
  3. The residual risk they accept

With a comment on the pull request: /sign-off accept or /sign-off reject.

  • Only the engineer assigned to the PR can sign.
  • The signature covers one commit. A new push needs a new review.
  • Accepting a BLOCK or NOT-FIX needs a written reason.
  • Signing unlocks the merge. It does not merge.
